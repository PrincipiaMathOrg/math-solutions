\begin{abstract}
On the house graph with uniform personalization, one vertex's
$\mu$-PageRank decreases and then increases. A six-vertex
theta graph has PageRank spread strictly increasing for $0<\mu<1$
and strictly decreasing for $\mu>1$. These give exact counterexamples
to both monotonicity assertions in Conjecture 3.1 of the cited
preprint, and also to the opposite spread direction suggested
by the source's prose. All graphs are connected, simple, undirected,
and have minimum degree two. Rational stationary distributions provide
certificates requiring only polynomial arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Task 1. Complete counterexamples}
We use Definition 2.4 of \cite{GJKO} exactly. For a graph with $n$
vertices, replace each edge by its two orientations. Write $y_{ij}$
for the stationary mass of oriented edge $(i,j)$, and $d_i$ for
the degree of vertex $i$. Fix damping $0<\epsilon<1$, backtracking
weight $\mu\ge0$, and uniform vertex personalization. Then
\[
 u_{ij}=\frac1{nd_i},\qquad
 y_{ij}=\frac{1-\epsilon}{nd_i}+
 \frac{\epsilon}{d_i-1+\mu}
 \left(\mu y_{ji}+\sum_{h\sim i,\ h\ne j}y_{hi}\right).          \tag{1}
\]
The vertex PageRank is the source's tail projection
\[
 \pi_\mu(i)=\sum_{j\sim i}y_{ij}.                              \tag{2}
\]
Every graph below has $d_i\ge2$, so (1) is defined even at $\mu=0$.
If $P_\mu$ is the column-stochastic transition matrix on oriented
edges, (1) is $y=\epsilon P_\mu y+(1-\epsilon)u$. It has the unique
solution
\[
 y=(1-\epsilon)\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^rP_\mu^r u,          \tag{3}
\]
because the series converges in the $\ell^1$ norm. This solution
is positive and has total mass one. Thus a normalized rational
vector satisfying (1) certifies the exact PageRank.

\begin{theorem}
The assertion that every coordinate of $\pi_\mu$ is monotonic in
$\mu$ is false, even for the five-vertex house graph with uniform
personalization and $\epsilon=1/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take vertices $0,1,2,3,4$ and edges
\[
 E_H=\{01,03,04,12,23,34\}.
\]
Vertices $0,3$ have degree three, and $1,2,4$ have degree two.
The reflection interchanges $0\leftrightarrow3$ and
$1\leftrightarrow2$, fixing $4$. Let
\[
 D=9\mu^3+87\mu^2+263\mu+241>0.
\]
The six directed-edge orbits have the following masses; each row
lists the numerator of the common denominator $30D$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
Mass & Directed edges & Numerator of the mass\\
\midrule
$a$ & $03,30$ & $6(\mu+5)(3\mu^2+16\mu+19)$\\
$b$ & $01,32$ & $3(7\mu^3+67\mu^2+201\mu+181)$\\
$c$ & $04,34$ & $21\mu^3+199\mu^2+595\mu+553$\\
$d$ & $10,23$ & $6(4\mu^3+39\mu^2+120\mu+113)$\\
$f$ & $12,21$ & $3(9\mu^3+85\mu^2+247\mu+211)$\\
$g$ & $40,43$ & $2(12\mu^3+115\mu^2+346\mu+319)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For a direct verification of the table, substitute its entries in
the six instances of (1):
\begin{align*}
 a&=\frac1{30}+\frac{\mu a+d+g}{2(\mu+2)},&
 b&=\frac1{30}+\frac{a+\mu d+g}{2(\mu+2)},\\
 c&=\frac1{30}+\frac{a+d+\mu g}{2(\mu+2)},&
 d&=\frac1{20}+\frac{\mu b+f}{2(\mu+1)},\\
 f&=\frac1{20}+\frac{b+\mu f}{2(\mu+1)},&
 g&=\frac1{20}+\frac c2.
\end{align*}
All six identities follow by multiplication by their denominators;
also $2(a+b+c+d+f+g)=1$. Positivity of the displayed coefficients
proves nonnegativity of every mass. Uniqueness in (3) identifies
this vector with the stationary distribution.

The roof vertex $4$ consequently has
\[
 p(\mu):=\pi_\mu(4)=2g
 =\frac{2(12\mu^3+115\mu^2+346\mu+319)}{15D}.                 \tag{4}
\]
In particular,
\[
 p(1/2)=\frac{8356}{47445},\qquad
 p(1)=\frac{22}{125},\qquad
 p(2)=\frac{3134}{17805},
\]
and the exact positive differences are
\[
 p(1/2)-p(1)=\frac{142}{1186125},\qquad
 p(2)-p(1)=\frac8{445125}.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Thus the coordinate is neither nondecreasing nor nonincreasing,
using three strictly positive backtracking parameters.
\end{proof}

For additional information, differentiation of (4) gives
\[
 p'(\mu)=\frac{2(9\mu^4+84\mu^3+206\mu^2-76\mu-511)}{15D^2}.
\]
Its numerator has exactly one positive zero: there is one sign
change in its coefficient sequence, while it is negative at $0$
and eventually positive. Hence this coordinate has exactly one
minimum for $\mu>0$.

\begin{theorem}
The PageRank spread need not be monotonic in either direction.
With uniform personalization and $\epsilon=9/10$, the graph
\[
 E_\Theta=\{01,04,05,12,23,34,35\}
\]
on $\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$ has spread strictly increasing on $[0,1]$
and strictly decreasing on $[1,\infty)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $A=\{0,3\}$, $B=\{1,2\}$ and $C=\{4,5\}$ be the vertex
orbits. The vertices in $A$ have degree three and the others
degree two. Write $a,b,c,d,f$ for the common masses of directed
edges of types $AB,AC,BA,BB,CA$, respectively. Their multiplicities
are $2,4,2,2,4$. Put
\[
 E=361\mu^2+10488\mu+59951>0.
\]
The following numerators over the common denominator $36E$ give
the stationary masses:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
Mass & Numerator\\
\midrule
$a$ & $893\mu^2+26754\mu+151753$\\
$b$ & $893\mu^2+25944\mu+152563$\\
$c$ & $12(76\mu^2+2283\mu+13091)$\\
$d$ & $3(361\mu^2+9918\mu+51521)$\\
$f$ & $12(76\mu^2+2208\mu+12941)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Indeed, these entries are positive, $2a+4b+2c+2d+4f=1$, and
direct substitution verifies (1), which now reduces to
\begin{align*}
 a&=\frac1{180}+\frac{9(\mu c+2f)}{10(\mu+2)},&
 b&=\frac1{180}+\frac{9(c+(\mu+1)f)}{10(\mu+2)},\\
 c&=\frac1{120}+\frac{9(\mu a+d)}{10(\mu+1)},&
 d&=\frac1{120}+\frac{9(a+\mu d)}{10(\mu+1)},\\
 f&=\frac1{120}+\frac{9b}{10}.&&
\end{align*}
The vertex scores are $\pi_A=a+2b$, $\pi_B=c+d$, $\pi_C=2f$:
\begin{align*}
 \pi_A&=\frac{893\mu^2+26214\mu+152293}{12E},\\
 \pi_B&=\frac{5(133\mu^2+3810\mu+20777)}{12E},\\
 \pi_C&=\frac{2(76\mu^2+2208\mu+12941)}{3E}.
\end{align*}
For all $\mu\ge0$, their order is strict, since
\[
 \pi_A-\pi_B=\frac{19\mu^2+597\mu+4034}{E}>0,\qquad
 \pi_B-\pi_C=\frac{19\mu^2+462\mu+119}{4E}>0.
\]
Thus the spread $s(\mu)=\max_i\pi_\mu(i)-\min_i\pi_\mu(i)$ is
\[
 s(\mu)=\frac{5(19\mu^2+570\mu+3251)}{4E},\qquad
 s'(\mu)=-\frac{855(\mu-1)(19\mu+221)}{2E^2}.                  \tag{6}
\]
This derivative proves both asserted strict inequalities. For
finite rational certificates one may also use
\[
 s(1)-s(1/2)=\frac{57}{20543092}>0,\qquad
 s(1)-s(2)=\frac{57}{6479852}>0.
\]
\end{proof}

The source's displayed spread inequality has the increasing
direction, while its original motivating prose describes a
shrinking range. Theorem 2 refutes both directions. Theorem 1
independently refutes coordinate monotonicity. The result therefore
does not depend on a sign or transcription discrepancy.

\section*{Tasks 2--5. Proposed follow-ups}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Task \arabic*.},start=2,leftmargin=*]
\item Characterize connected graphs for which every coordinate
is monotonic in $\mu$ for uniform personalization and every damping
parameter in $(0,1)$.
\item For the house and theta graphs, determine exactly how the
number and location of turning points vary with damping and with
the personalization vector.
\item Bound the number of positive critical points of one
$\mu$-PageRank coordinate in terms of graph size or the number of
orbits of directed edges.
\item Find conditions under which $\infty$-PageRank still correctly
predicts the ordering between standard and non-backtracking scores,
without assuming monotonicity along the whole parameter interval.
\end{enumerate}
