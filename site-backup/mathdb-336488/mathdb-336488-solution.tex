\begin{abstract}
Conjecture 7.2 of Teknomo's \emph{Premagic and Ideal Flow Matrices}
asserts that every premagic matrix is diagonalizable. We give a positive
integer counterexample of order three, the least possible real order.
Its zero eigenvalue has algebraic multiplicity two and geometric
multiplicity one. Scaling gives a positive doubly stochastic counterexample;
adding a positive multiple of the identity gives invertible ones.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Task 1: the original conjecture}
Let $\mathbf 1$ be the column vector of ones. A real square matrix $A$
is \emph{premagic} when its vector of row sums equals its vector of
column sums, that is,
\[
 A\mathbf 1=A^T\mathbf 1.
\]
This is Definition 4.3 of the final article \cite{Teknomo}, page 37.
Conjecture 7.2, page 44, asserts that such a matrix is diagonalizable
and has a full set of independent eigenvectors. The same conjecture
appears in the current arXiv manuscript. The definition imposes no
zero-diagonal condition. Our example is strictly positive, so it also
satisfies nonnegativity and irreducibility if these properties are
additionally desired in the ideal-flow setting.

\begin{theorem}
The positive integer matrix
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}4&4&1\\2&2&5\\3&3&3\end{pmatrix}
\]
is premagic but is not diagonalizable, even over $\mathbb C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every row sum and every column sum is $9$. Thus $M$ is premagic.
Put
\[
 j=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These vectors form a basis: the matrix $B=(j\ u\ v)$ has determinant
$6$. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 Mj=9j,\qquad Mu=0,\qquad Mv=6u.
\]
Consequently
\[
 B^{-1}MB=
 \begin{pmatrix}9&0&0\\0&0&6\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The characteristic polynomial is $x^2(x-9)$. The eigenspace for $0$
is exactly $\mathbb C u$ and that for $9$ is exactly $\mathbb C j$.
There are only two independent eigenvectors, although the dimension
is three. This disproves both assertions of the conjecture.
\end{proof}

The construction can also be written $M=3jj^T+uv^T$.
The equalities $j^Tu=j^Tv=v^Tu=0$ make $uv^T$ a nonzero square-zero
operator on $j^\perp$, while $3jj^T$ acts by $9$ on $j$ and vanishes
on $j^\perp$. Hence the positive dominant eigenvalue is simple, exactly
as expected from Perron--Frobenius theory; the defect occurs at the
other eigenvalue.

\begin{proposition}
The matrix $M/9$ is a strictly positive doubly stochastic matrix that
is not diagonalizable. For every real $t>0$, the matrix $M+tI$ is
strictly positive, invertible, premagic, and not diagonalizable.
No real premagic matrix of order less than three is defective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Dividing by $9$ makes all row and column sums one and preserves the
eigenspaces and the nontrivial Jordan block. Adding $tI$ changes the
eigenvalues to $9+t,t,t$ and preserves the same block and eigenvectors;
its determinant is $(9+t)t^2>0$ and its row and column sums are $9+t$.
For order two, write $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The premagic condition gives $a+b=a+c$, so $b=c$. Thus $A$ is real
symmetric and diagonalizable. Order one is immediate.
\end{proof}

\section*{Verification and scope}
The proof uses only exact matrix identities. The accompanying
standard-library Python verifier checks the row and column sums,
$\det B=6$, the similarity relation $MB=BJ$, the characteristic
polynomial coefficients, and the scaled and shifted identities using
integer and rational arithmetic. No floating-point eigensolver is used.
This resolves MathDB 336488 as a counterexample to the full universal
statement. The minimal-order assertion here concerns real matrices.
The source and bounded prior-claim search are documented separately.
The review is by the author agent, without independent peer review.

\section*{Four follow-up tasks}
These are proposed problems, not additional solved credits.
\begin{description}
\item[Task 2.] Determine the least total entry sum of a strictly positive
integer $3\times3$ premagic matrix that is not diagonalizable over
$\mathbb C$, and classify all minimizers up to simultaneous permutation
of rows and columns.
\item[Task 3.] Determine the least order of a nonnegative, irreducible,
zero-diagonal premagic matrix that is not diagonalizable over
$\mathbb C$, and provide an exact counterexample at that order or prove
that no such matrix exists.
\item[Task 4.] For every $n\ge3$, characterize the lists of complex
eigenvalues and Jordan block sizes realizable by strictly positive
doubly stochastic $n\times n$ matrices.
\item[Task 5.] Give an explicit parametrization of all defective real
$3\times3$ premagic matrices, including necessary and sufficient
inequalities on the parameters for every entry to be strictly positive.
\end{description}
